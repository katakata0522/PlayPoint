Do US Google Play Points promotions stack? Earn-rate rules

United States guide ・ 2026-08-05

For one eligible purchase, Google says multiple promotions cannot be combined. The highest available earning rate applies. A direct offer such as 3 points per $1 is not added to Silver's 1.1 points per $1.

Use the highest eligible rate

If a Silver account has its regular 1.1 points per $1 rate and an eligible 3 points per $1 offer, use 3 points per $1, not 4.1. Two visible offers also do not become 5x or 6x.

The checkout or offer terms decide which rate is eligible. Account-specific offers may differ even when two users open the same app page.

Read 'x' and direct-rate labels differently

A label saying '3x your regular rate' is a multiplier whose base is defined by the offer. A label saying '3 points per $1' is already the final direct rate.

Do not convert the two labels into the same formula without reading the terms. The Play Store preview is the safest final check.

Tax, rounding and split purchases

Points are calculated from the item price excluding tax, and each item is rounded to the nearest whole point. A cart total estimate can therefore differ from the sum of item results.

Splitting a purchase can change rounding, but it does not create a guaranteed advantage. Calculate the actual purchase units and verify Points history afterward.

Activation, period and caps

Some offers must be activated before purchase and can limit apps, users, dates or maximum bonus points. A screenshot of an offer does not prove the current account qualifies.

Use the highest eligible displayed rate in the calculator, then keep the offer terms until the points appear.

FAQ

Does Silver 1.1 plus a 3-point offer become 4.1 points per $1?

No. The higher eligible rate applies; the rates are not added.

Do 2x and 3x become 6x?

No. Multiple promotions do not combine on one purchase.

Are taxes included in the point calculation?

No. Google calculates from item price excluding tax.
