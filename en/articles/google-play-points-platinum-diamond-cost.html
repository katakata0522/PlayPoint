Compare the official US conditions

For the United States, Google lists Platinum at 3,000–9,999 points and Diamond at 10,000 points or more. The base earning rates are 1.4 points per $1 at Platinum and 1.6 points per $1 at Diamond.

Normal-rate maintenance estimates

If an account starts the year already at that level and every eligible purchase earns only the level's base rate, 3,000 Platinum points correspond to about $2,143 of eligible spending. At Diamond, 10,000 points correspond to $6,250.

Platinum: 3,000 ÷ 1.4 ≈ $2,143
Diamond: 10,000 ÷ 1.6 = $6,250

These are maintenance references, not universal costs to reach a level from Bronze, Silver, or Gold. While moving up, the base rate shown for the current level applies until the account actually advances. Use the missing points and current rate shown in Google Play for a personal estimate.

Why actual points can differ

Google calculates points from the eligible item price and the account's base earning rate, then rounds to the nearest whole point. Taxes are not included. Per-purchase rounding means an annual total divided once may not exactly match the activity history.

A promotion can improve the result only when the exact purchase qualifies. Promotions can have dates, app limits, activation steps, currencies, or other conditions, and more than one promotion cannot be stacked on the same purchase.

Weekly prizes and premium support

The US official level table lists weekly prizes and premium support for both Platinum and Diamond. That means premium support is not a Diamond-only benefit.

Google says support users can be prioritized and matched with a dedicated team, but waiting times are not guaranteed and benefits are subject to availability. Weekly prize outcomes also should not be treated as guaranteed cash value.

When Platinum may be reasonable

Platinum becomes a practical comparison when purchases you already planned put the account close to 3,000 points and you actually use the weekly prizes or support. The level should follow normal use, not create new spending.

If reaching it requires subscriptions, apps, or in-game items you would not otherwise buy, staying at the current level is the clearer financial result.

When Diamond is worth calculating

Diamond is worth a closer estimate when normal annual purchases already put you near 10,000 points and the higher base rate will be used repeatedly. Start with the remaining points shown by Google Play, not the redeemable balance.

If the estimated amount is far above the normal annual budget, that gap is the answer. A multiplier event can improve the timing of a purchase you already intended to make, but it should not invent a purchase.

Check in this order before buying

  1. Open Google Play and record the current level and points still needed.
  2. Check the base earning rate on the Earn page.
  3. Run the calculator with the normal rate first.
  4. Compare a promotion only if the purchase clearly qualifies.
  5. Stop treating the level as a goal if the amount exceeds the planned budget.

FAQ

Is Diamond always better than Platinum?

No. Diamond has a higher base earning rate, but it also requires far more annual points. The practical choice depends on whether normal planned purchases already put you near the requirement.

Does spending $6,250 guarantee Diamond maintenance?

No. It is a simple reference for an account already at Diamond if all eligible purchases earn the US Diamond base rate. Taxes, per-purchase rounding, eligibility, refunds, and promotions can change the result.

Do both levels include premium support?

Yes, the US official level table lists premium support for both Platinum and Diamond. Google does not guarantee waiting times, and support benefits are subject to availability.

Does redeeming points reduce level progress?

Google says redeeming points reduces the usable balance, not level progress. A returned or cancelled purchase can deduct points from both.
