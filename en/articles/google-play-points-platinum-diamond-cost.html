US Play Points: Platinum starts at 3,000, Diamond at 10,000 annual points

Updated 2026-08-05 ・ US official conditions

For a US Google Play account, Platinum covers 3,000–9,999 annual points and Diamond starts at 10,000. Their base rates are 1.4 and 1.6 points per $1. The right comparison is not “which badge is better,” but whether purchases you already plan put the account close enough to use the difference.

Maintenance references are not universal “costs to reach”

If the account starts the year already at the level and every eligible purchase earns only that level's base rate, 3,000 Platinum points correspond to about $2,143 and 10,000 Diamond points to $6,250.

Platinum: 3,000 ÷ 1.4 ≈ $2,143
Diamond: 10,000 ÷ 1.6 = $6,250

From a lower level, the current level's rate applies until the account advances. Taxes, item eligibility, per-purchase rounding, refunds, and promotions also change the result. Calculate from the missing annual points shown in Google Play.

Do not divide every remaining point by the destination level’s rate unless the account already earns at that rate. Use the maintenance calculators for a same-level annual reference, and use the status calculator when progress still crosses lower levels.

Who may benefit from each level

Platinum

Worth comparing when planned annual purchases already put the account near 3,000 points and you use weekly prizes or premium support.

Diamond

Worth calculating when planned annual purchases already approach 10,000 points and the higher 1.6-point rate will apply repeatedly after the level is reached.

Neither as a target

When the remaining amount is far above the planned Google Play budget, the current level is the more accurate financial result. A multiplier can improve the timing of a planned purchase, but it does not create a guaranteed return.

Check in this order

  1. Record the current level and annual points still needed.
  2. Confirm the base rate in the Earn tab.
  3. Calculate the normal-rate result first.
  4. Add a promotion only when the exact purchase qualifies.
  5. Compare the remaining amount with the planned annual budget.
