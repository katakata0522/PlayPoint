Play Points app install reward not received? What to check

US reference. Offers depend on your Google Play country and account.

Updated 2026-10-14 Date details

Published October 13, 2026 · Updated October 14, 2026 · Official info checked October 14, 2026 · Play Points guide

Saved articles & reading history

Installed the app but still waiting for points?

When an install bonus doesn’t show up right away, check Google’s two timing clocks first. On US accounts, a featured install requires a true first-time download plus at least two minutes of app use within the first 24 hours—and even after you finish that cleanly, points can take up to seven days to arrive.

Those two windows do completely separate jobs: 24 hours to try the app, and up to a week for the reward to post. Before trying another download, match what you did against Google’s US install-offer conditions.

Start with the exact offer card you saw

Open the Google Play Store on the exact Google Account that showed the promo, then head to Play Points → Earn. If the card is still visible, double-check the exact app name, point amount, expiration date, and any activation step—and take a quick screenshot so you have the original wording handy.

Just downloading a free app from search never pays Play Points on its own; the install has to start from an eligible offer card. If the card actually asks for multiple gameplay tasks, a purchase, or a subscription rather than a simple install-and-open, switch to our Google Play quest guide instead.

Suppose you install a featured game at 9:00 a.m. on Monday—you need to open and actively use it for at least two minutes before 9:00 a.m. on Tuesday. Playing for five minutes on Monday afternoon qualifies; waiting until Wednesday to launch it misses the 24-hour activity window, because the seven-day wait only covers point delivery.

If you hit that two-minute window in time, give Google the full seven days before assuming something broke. Points history won’t list an entry right away while the reward is still processing, though ineligible attempts won’t show up there either.

Why a reinstall still counts as a previous install

Pause and check whether you ever downloaded that app before—even years ago on an older phone under the same Google Account. As Google’s guidance on new-app offers notes, eligibility relies on prior download, installation, and usage records, so deleting an app and installing it again won’t qualify as a first-time install.

Hold onto your original download and playtime timestamps too; reinstalling the app a second time to “retry” the offer won’t reset eligibility, and support will want the first install record.

How Play personalization affects install tracking

Google uses app-activity signals connected to your personalization settings to verify that you met an install offer’s two-minute play requirement. Changing personalization controls can affect whether future install offers track properly, though toggling a setting afterward won’t rescue an expired promo.

In the Play Store, open Profile → Personalization in Play to see how those controls work. Keep in mind that Play personalization, Play History, and your Play Games profile do separate jobs—Google notes that turning off Play History doesn’t interrupt Play Points membership or quest tracking, though individual install offers still rely on activity verification.

Before adjusting any data controls, review Google’s personalization and history explanation so you know what each setting affects.

What to check—and send support—if points stay missing

On the same Google Account, go to Play Store → Profile → Play Points → More → Points history and look specifically for the install offer line item rather than guessing from your total point balance. Our balance and history guide breaks down how those screens line up.

If seven full days have passed and every rule on the card looks met, reach out through Google Play support with a concise summary:

  • The app name, offer title, expected points, and original card wording (or screenshot).
  • The date and approximate time you installed and played the app.
  • What your Points history currently shows and the date you checked it.
  • Any error message or account setting change tied to the offer.

Keep your screenshots and account info inside the official Google support chat or form rather than posting them publicly—having your exact timeline ready makes it much faster for an agent to trace.

If your missing points came from a paid app or in-game purchase instead of a free install promo, switch over to our purchase points not showing up checklist (and don’t make another test purchase just to check).
